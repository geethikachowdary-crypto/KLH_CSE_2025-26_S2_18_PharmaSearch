
DOSAGE INFORMATION

Introduction to Dosage Information

Dosage information describes the amount, frequency, method,
and duration of medicine use according to the relevant product
instructions or a qualified healthcare professional's guidance.
It is an important part of medicine information because different
products may have different strengths, formulations, and
administration requirements.

Dosage information should always be interpreted in the context
of the specific medicine, its approved use, and the individual
patient. A general dosage statement in a text file is not
sufficient to determine a safe or appropriate dose for a person.

Strength and Dose

Strength and dose are related but different concepts. Strength
describes the quantity of an active ingredient in a particular
unit or volume of a product. Dose refers to the amount used or
administered according to the relevant instructions.

For example, a product may be described by the amount of active
ingredient in one tablet or a specified volume of liquid.
The actual dose and frequency depend on the product, purpose,
and professional instructions.

Dosage Form

Medicines are available in different dosage forms. Common forms
include tablets, capsules, syrups, creams, ointments, injections,
and inhalers. Each form may have specific administration
requirements.

A tablet should not automatically be treated as equivalent to a
liquid or an extended-release formulation. Some products have
special release characteristics or handling instructions.
Users should follow the approved product information and seek
professional guidance when they have questions.

Frequency and Duration

Frequency describes how often a medicine is used. Duration
describes how long the treatment is continued. These instructions
depend on the medicine, its purpose, and the patient's clinical
circumstances.

Users should not independently increase the frequency, extend
the treatment period, or change the dose because of a search
result. If an instruction is unclear, the user should consult a
pharmacist or healthcare professional.

Routes of Administration

The route of administration identifies how a medicine enters
the body or is applied. Examples include oral, topical,
inhaled, and injectable routes. Different routes can result in
different effects and administration requirements.

A medicine intended for one route should not automatically be
used through another route. Product-specific instructions and
professional guidance are important for safe administration.

Storage and Handling

Some medicines require specific storage conditions. Information
may include temperature requirements, protection from light,
moisture precautions, or special handling instructions.

Storage requirements depend on the product. Users should follow
the original label and official product information rather
than relying on general assumptions.

Missed Doses and Safety

Questions about missed doses, side effects, medicine
interactions, allergies, or dosage changes should be discussed
with an appropriate healthcare professional. The correct action
may depend on the medicine, the prescribed schedule, and the
patient's circumstances.

The Pharma Search application does not calculate personalized
dosage recommendations or decide whether a user should take a
particular medicine.

Role in the Project

The dosage_information.txt file stores general dosage-related
terminology and educational information. It is read by the
Java program along with the other 10 text files.

The search system extracts words, converts them to lowercase,
and calculates Levenshtein Edit Distance between the entered
search word and the extracted words. It applies a simple
threshold and sorts possible matches.

For example, a user may search for a word such as dosage,
strength, frequency, or administration. The system can identify
close textual matches, including some spelling variations.

Project Limitations

The file is intended for demonstrating Java file handling and
approximate text search. It does not replace medicine labels,
prescriptions, clinical guidelines, or professional advice.

Actual dosage decisions require reliable, product-specific
information and appropriate clinical assessment.
