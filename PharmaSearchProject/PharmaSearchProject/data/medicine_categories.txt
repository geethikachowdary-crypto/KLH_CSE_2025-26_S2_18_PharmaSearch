
MEDICINE CATEGORIES

Introduction to Medicine Categories

Medicine categories are used to organize medicines according to
their therapeutic purpose, active ingredients, pharmacological
characteristics, or intended clinical applications. Organizing
medicine information into categories makes it easier to manage
records, search for terminology, and understand the general
purpose of different products.

A single medicine may be associated with more than one
classification depending on its active ingredient, approved
uses, and formulation. A category provides general information
and should not be used by itself to determine whether a medicine
is appropriate for a particular patient.

Pain Relief Medicines

Pain relief medicines are associated with the management of
different types of pain. Products in this broad group may
contain different active ingredients and may have different
warnings and safety requirements. The cause, severity, and
duration of pain can affect the appropriate approach to
treatment.

A medicine information system may store terms such as pain,
analgesic, inflammation, tablet, and active ingredient. These
terms can help demonstrate how the search program identifies
words from a category-related text file.

Antibiotics

Antibiotics are medicines used to treat certain bacterial
infections. They are not effective against viral infections
such as most common colds. The selection of an antibiotic
depends on the infection, patient-specific factors, and
appropriate professional assessment.

Incorrect or unnecessary antibiotic use can contribute to
antimicrobial resistance. Therefore, information about an
antibiotic should be interpreted carefully. A search result
containing an antibiotic-related word does not establish that
the medicine is needed or suitable for a user.

Antacids and Digestive Medicines

Antacids and other digestive medicines are associated with the
management of selected digestive symptoms and conditions.
Different products can work in different ways and may contain
different ingredients. Product instructions and professional
guidance should be followed.

Common searchable terms may include acidity, stomach, digestive,
antacid, tablet, liquid, and ingredient. These terms are useful
for demonstrating approximate text matching in the project.

Antihistamines

Antihistamines are medicines commonly associated with the
management of allergic symptoms. Different antihistamines can
have different effects and precautions. Some products may cause
drowsiness, while others have different side-effect profiles.

A medicine category file can include words such as allergy,
antihistamine, itching, reaction, and symptoms. The search system
can compare these words with a user's entered search term.

Antidiabetic Medicines

Antidiabetic medicines are used in the management of diabetes.
The treatment approach depends on the type of diabetes and
individual clinical circumstances. Medicines can have different
mechanisms of action, administration instructions, and
monitoring requirements.

Searchable terms may include glucose, diabetes, insulin,
monitoring, and treatment. The system only identifies words
that appear in its text files and does not calculate or recommend
a treatment plan.

Antihypertensive Medicines

Antihypertensive medicines are used to manage high blood
pressure. Different medicines act through different mechanisms,
and treatment decisions require appropriate medical evaluation.
The dosage and choice of medicine should not be changed without
professional guidance.

Other Categories

Additional medicine categories include antifungal medicines,
antiviral medicines, respiratory medicines, dermatological
preparations, and nutritional products. Each category may
contain products with different purposes and safety
considerations.

Role in Pharma Search

The medicine categories file is used as a searchable text
resource in the Pharma Search application. The program reads
the file, extracts individual words, converts them to lowercase,
and compares them with the user's search word using Levenshtein
Edit Distance.

The system applies a threshold and displays a limited number
of close matches. This demonstrates how approximate searching
can support text retrieval.

Medicine categories are intended for educational organization
in this project. They do not replace approved product
information or professional medical advice.
